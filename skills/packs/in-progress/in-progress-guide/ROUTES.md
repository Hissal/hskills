# Routes

Every route here is a trial: label it experimental. Pick by the situation, not by the name that sounds closest.

| Situation | Invocation | Useful difference | Established alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Design or specify a recurring workflow, without executing it | `/loop-me` | Grills through a recurring-work lens (what triggers each run, where the human checks in, what they are shown) and keeps notes on the user's world across sessions; its only output is workflow specs, each done when an implementer could build it without asking a question | `/grilling` to settle the design, then `/to-spec` to write it up |
| Take over an existing PR's checks, conflicts and review feedback until it is ready, without merging | `/babysit-pr <PR>` | Owns the PR through every round on its own: waits on checks and on whichever review bots the repository uses, through the host's PR monitoring or a bundled watcher, fixes CI and conflicts, handles each finding through `receiving-review`, and stops at a stated done bar with a report; adds no authority of its own, never merges, and on someone else's PR never rewrites their history. `/full-stop` and `/full-send` tend their PRs through it | `/receiving-review` on each round of feedback, watching checks and conflicts yourself |
| Take a spec or a rough goal to a PR ready for you to merge, unattended, without merging it | `/full-stop <goal>` | Runs the whole way on its own: frames a rough goal with no approval stop and records its consequential assumptions, proves the change through `verifying`, opens the PR and tends its checks, conflicts and review rounds to a stated done bar, then reports a verdict; never merges, and turns off auto-merge and leaves any merge queue | `/implement` on a spec or tickets (`/implement-spec` for a whole spec and its tickets), then tending the PR's checks and reviews yourself |
| Take a spec or a rough goal all the way to a merged PR, unattended, under the project's own merge requirements | `/full-send <goal>` | Runs the same unattended procedure as `/full-stop`, then merges: invoking it is the merge authorization, so it never asks again, but it merges only on the latest head once the done bar holds and GitHub reports the PR mergeable, with the repository's own method (staying until a merge queue or auto-merge reports it merged); it never bypasses a required approval or weakens a check, and a required human review comes back to you as the one thing left | `/full-stop <goal>` to stop at a ready PR you merge yourself; `/implement` (`/implement-spec` for a whole spec) and then merging by hand |
| Compare two independently built implementations of one goal and keep the stronger | `/arena` | Two implementers build the same goal under the same constraints and criteria, each in isolation and on another model family when one is available; a separate evaluator runs each criterion's check on both artifacts, picks a base, may carry over the other's ideas, and the result is verified. Costs about two builds plus an evaluation | `/implement` for one build; `/prototype` to compare design variants without building the real thing |
| Have several models judge and score a piece of work, once or in rounds of fixes until they are satisfied | `/council <target>` | Three members, one flagship model from each of three providers, comment on and score the same target in fresh contexts: a change, a document, a plan, or candidates to compare. In Review it judges once and changes nothing; in Loop it fixes what it accepts and asks again, up to 3 rounds unless you set another, keeping scope to the original request; scores go in a per-round table, and in the PR description when the work ends in one. Costs three flagship reviews per round | `/second-opinion` for one fresh reader from another model family; `/code-review` for one reviewer per review axis |
